14 Best Stock Photography Sites to Sell Your Photos

June 1, 2026 - 4 min read

Stock photography offers one of the most accessible ways to earn passive income from your images. But choosing the right platform makes the difference between occasional pocket money and a steady revenue stream. Below are the top stock sites ranked by earnings potential, exclusivity terms, and ease of getting accepted.

The Best Platforms for Contributors

Vecteezy offers a 50% revenue split on Pro sales and pays $5 per 1,000 downloads for free images. It's non-exclusive, so you can upload the same work elsewhere.

Getty Images is the largest and most prestigious agency with over 200 million assets. Images sell for $150–$499 depending on size, but contributors keep only 20% of each sale. Getting accepted is competitive.

Depositphotos uses a tiered royalty system. New contributors earn 34%, and top earners reach 42%. The more you sell, the higher your tier.

iStock (owned by Getty) offers 15% commission for non-exclusive content and up to 45% for exclusive content. Non-exclusive is flexible but pays significantly less per sale.

Dreamstime pays 25%–50% depending on exclusivity and download volume. It's beginner-friendly with a relatively straightforward acceptance process.

Stocksy stands out for its artist-friendly model. Contributors receive 50% commission on standard licenses and 75% on extended licenses. Acceptance is highly selective, but the payout structure is one of the best in the industry.

Shutterstock remains one of the most popular microstock platforms with 270 million images. The monthly subscription model means individual image earnings are low, but volume is high. Commission starts at 25%.

Adobe Stock (formerly Fotolia) pays a flat 33% on photos and 35% on video. Integration with Adobe's ecosystem gives contributors exposure to a large creative customer base.

Pond5 lets contributors set their own prices and keeps a 50% cut. It's best known for video but also sells stills.

Creative Market earns contributors 70% on each sale and allows you to set your own prices. With 5 million members browsing, it offers strong exposure.

Honorable Mentions

Cavan Images (50% commission, exclusive only), Offset (Shutterstock's premium brand, invitation-preferred), Westend61 (40–60% commission for curated work), and Yay Images (flat 50% commission, free tier available) are strong niche options worth exploring if your style fits their collections.

Practical Tips

  • Start with non-exclusive platforms like Vecteezy and Shutterstock while you build your portfolio.
  • Submit your strongest 10–20 images for review, not your entire archive. Quality over quantity.
  • Keyword thoroughly. Most buyers find images through search, and accurate metadata is the difference between zero downloads and regular sales.
  • Photograph subjects with commercial value: business, lifestyle, technology, food, and travel consistently outsell abstract and fine-art categories.
  • Read each platform's license terms carefully. Some agencies require exclusivity for their best rates.

Final Thoughts

Stock photography is not a get-rich-quick path. The photographers who earn consistently treat it as a long-term library build — uploading regularly, studying what sells, and adapting their style to what buyers actually need. The best platform for you is the one where your images fit naturally and the commission structure rewards your effort.

FAQ

How much can I realistically earn from stock photography? Most contributors earn anywhere from a few dollars a month to a few thousand. Top earners upload thousands of images across multiple platforms and treat it as a dedicated income stream.

Should I upload to one site or many? Non-exclusive platforms let you distribute the same images across multiple sites, increasing your reach. The exception is if you qualify for a high-percentage exclusive deal with a premium agency.

What subjects sell best on stock sites? Business, lifestyle, food, technology, and travel consistently perform well. Authentic, diverse, and candid-style images currently outperform heavily staged or clichéd stock looks.

How long does it take to start earning? It varies. Some contributors make their first sale within weeks; others wait months. Consistency in uploading new work is the most reliable way to build momentum.
